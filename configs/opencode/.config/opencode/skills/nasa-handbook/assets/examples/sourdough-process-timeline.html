LEVAIN BREAD PRODUCTION SEQUENCE
2.5.1

INTRODUCTION.

The levain bread production sequence turns flour, water, salt and a ripe levain into two 900 g (2.0 lb) country loaves in 27 hours. Fermentation does the work, not the baker: the baker is in attendance for about 55 minutes, and a 16-hour cold retard at 3 °C (38 °F) carries the shaped loaf over to a morning bake (figure 2.5-1).

2.5.2

FUNCTIONAL DESCRIPTION.

At T = 0 (08:00) the levain is built 1:2:2 and held at 24 °C (75 °F) for 5 hours to peak. Flour and water autolyse 1 hour; levain and salt are then mixed in. Bulk fermentation runs 4 hours with three stretch-and-fold sets. The dough is divided, preshaped, shaped into a BANNETON and retarded, then baked in a DUTCH OVEN preheated to 232 °C (450 °F) (figure 2.5-2).

2.5.2.1

Lid Steam and Bake Completion.

The DUTCH OVEN LID traps the loaf's own steam for 20 minutes while it rises, then comes off for 30 minutes of browning. The bake is done at Tcore ≥ 97 °C (207 °F) on the PROBE.

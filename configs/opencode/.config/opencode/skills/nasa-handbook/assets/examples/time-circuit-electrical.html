TEMPORAL DISPLACEMENT CIRCUIT
2.6.1

INTRODUCTION.

The temporal displacement circuit (TDC) transfers the vehicle and its occupants to the date set on the TIME CIRCUITS. Displacement occurs only when the flux dispersal assembly receives a 1.21-gigawatt pulse while the vehicle is moving at 88 mph (141.6 km/h) or more (figure 2.6-1).

2.6.2

FUNCTIONAL DESCRIPTION.

With TIME CIRCUITS on, the driver enters the destination on the keypad (figure 2.6-2). FLUX MODE at STBY enables the primary power unit (PPU), which charges discharge bank C1 through reactor L1 in 6 sec; the FLUX CHG talkback goes from barber pole to gray. At ARM, the speed comparator energizes relay K1 at 88 mph, and contactor K2 dumps C1 into the three electrode tubes of the flux dispersal assembly.

2.6.2.1

Arming Interlock.

K1 closes only at 88 mph or more with a destination set. C1 releases 1.21 GW × 12 msec = 14.5 MJ; a lightning strike on the roof hook may charge C1 in place of the PPU.
